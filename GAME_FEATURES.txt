NEON RIFT: THE LAST GUARDIAN
DETAILED GAME FEATURES
========================================


1. 3D CYBERPUNK ENVIRONMENT
----------------------------

The game is presented as a first-person 3D cyberpunk survival shooter built
with PyOpenGL, GLU, and GLUT. The ruined city contains:

- Collapsed buildings and destroyed roads.
- Rubble, cliffs, bridges, and solid environmental objects.
- Trees, corrupted vegetation, rocks, grass, and other decorations.
- Mechanical spike traps and unstable field hazards.
- A fixed Aether Grid Core at the center of the mission.

The environment is generated from a grid-based map layout. The map controls
walkable areas, obstacles, enemy spawn locations, crystal locations, traps,
and the Core position. Buildings, rubble, and trees affect player movement,
enemy navigation, and projectile travel, allowing the player to use cover.


2. MATHEMATICS, PHYSICS, AND SIMULATION
---------------------------------------

The game uses mathematical calculations for movement, aiming, combat,
collision detection, enemy behavior, pickups, equipment ranges, and visual
animation.

Distance and vector calculations:
    Euclidean distance is calculated from coordinate differences using:

        distance = sqrt(dx^2 + dy^2)

    Squared distance checks are used for range comparisons such as EMP radius,
    enemy detection, pickup collection, and collision checks.

Trigonometric aiming:
    Player yaw and pitch are converted from degrees to radians. Sine and
    cosine generate the forward direction used for movement and shooting.
    The atan2 function calculates enemy headings and the direction displayed
    by the objective arrow.

Crosshair targeting:
    A dot product compares the player's forward direction with the direction
    to an enemy. This determines whether an enemy is close enough to the
    crosshair to display its health bar and provide targeting feedback.

Gravity and jumping:
    Jumping uses vertical physics. A jump begins with a vertical velocity of
    430 units per second. Gravity reduces the velocity by 980 units per second
    squared. Player height and vertical velocity are updated with elapsed
    frame time until the player reaches the ground.

    The landing system clamps the player's height to zero, resets vertical
    velocity, and allows the next jump. Crouching changes the eye height and
    movement speed.

Collision and movement:
    The player's collision area is treated as a circle. Center and surrounding
    sample points are checked against solid map cells and procedural tree
    trunks. Movement is divided into small substeps to prevent the player from
    passing through walls during slow frames. Separate X and Y checks allow
    smooth sliding along blocked surfaces.

Frame-rate independence:
    The game uses delta time so movement, physics, projectiles, cooldowns,
    spawning, damage, lifetimes, and objectives behave consistently across
    different frame rates. A maximum update step prevents unstable physics
    after a pause or a slow frame.

Animation mathematics:
    Sine and cosine functions animate floating crystals, Rift Anchors, enemy
    hovering, trap cycles, weapon bob, weapon sway, particles, visual pulses,
    and other timed effects.


3. MISSION AND OBJECTIVE SYSTEM
-------------------------------

The mission has a clear progression:

1. Protect the Aether Grid Core.
2. Find and destroy three armed Rift Anchors.
3. Collect the three ECHO memory crystals unlocked by the Anchors.
4. Return to the Core and complete the final Core link.
5. Choose ECHO's fate after the link reaches 100%.

The objective arrow points toward the nearest unfinished crystal and later
points back to the Grid Core. Collecting a crystal updates the objective and
creates a new respawn checkpoint.


4. RIFT ANCHORS AND MEMORY CRYSTALS
------------------------------------

There are three magenta Rift Anchors and three pink ECHO memory crystals.
Each Anchor has 5 health and protects one crystal with a shield.

- Anchors launch corruption projectiles at the player.
- Anchor projectiles cause 14 damage.
- The Anchor shield disappears after the Anchor is destroyed.
- A destroyed Anchor triggers a new enemy wave.
- Anchor fire becomes faster as the mission progresses.
- Each collected crystal awards 50 points.
- Each collected crystal creates a checkpoint.

The player must shoot an Anchor's glowing heart five times before its matching
memory crystal can be collected.


5. COMBAT AND RISK MANAGEMENT
-----------------------------

The player fires energy projectiles using the center crosshair and first-person
aiming controls.

- A successful enemy hit awards 10 points.
- An Anchor hit awards 15 points.
- Destroying an Anchor awards an additional 100 points.
- Brute Rift Beasts require more hits than standard enemies.
- Projectiles can collide with enemies, Anchors, trees, buildings, rubble,
  and other environmental objects.

Missed shots create a strategic risk. A projectile that expires or hits scenery
without striking an enemy or Anchor reduces Grid integrity by 4%. If Grid
integrity reaches zero, the mission ends.


6. ENEMY AI AND DYNAMIC DIFFICULTY
----------------------------------

Rift Beasts use detection, pursuit, navigation, attack, and Core-targeting
behaviors.

Standard Rift Beast:
    A fast corrupted creature that can chase Aegis-7 or travel toward the
    Grid Core. It has animated movement and alternating claw attacks.

Brute Rift Beast:
    A larger and stronger enemy with more health and greater attack damage.

Enemy behavior includes:

- Detecting and pursuing the player.
- Becoming aggressive after being attacked.
- Navigating around solid map areas.
- Attacking the player at close range.
- Travelling toward and damaging the Grid Core.
- Rotating toward targets with smooth heading changes.
- Respawning after defeat.
- Showing health bars when targeted or recently damaged.

Enemies use breadth-first-search navigation fields to find routes around
buildings and other blocked cells instead of moving directly through them.

The game begins with 10 active enemies. Enemy speed, strength, spawn frequency,
and population increase as time passes. Destroying each Anchor adds two more
enemies to the target population. The final Core link increases the target to
20 hostiles and adds another speed boost.


7. GUARDIAN SUPPORT EQUIPMENT
-----------------------------

EMP Pulse — Q
    The player has 3 EMP charges. Each pulse stuns nearby Rift Beasts for
    3.6 seconds and clears hostile projectiles within its radius. EMP does not
    damage enemies and has an 8-second recharge.

Auto-Sentry — E
    The player has 2 deployable auto-sentries. Each sentry remains active for
    48 seconds, automatically aims at visible enemies, and fires within range.
    Buildings, trees, and other obstacles block its line of sight.

EMP and sentry charges are limited for the entire mission and are not restored
after respawning. Their use is intended to be strategic, especially during
Anchor fights and the final Core defense.


8. CORE DEFENSE AND FINAL LINK
------------------------------

The Aether Grid Core is a fixed structure with independent health. Rift Beasts
can attack it while the player is exploring other areas.

After all three memories are collected, returning close to the Core starts the
final 24-second Core link. A large green signal ring marks the required area.

- Link progress increases while the player remains inside the ring.
- Link progress decreases when the player leaves the ring.
- Enemy pressure reaches its maximum during the link.
- The ending choice appears only after progress reaches 100%.


9. PLAYER SURVIVAL AND PROGRESSION
----------------------------------

Aegis-7 starts with 100 HP and 3 lives. Enemy attacks and active spike traps
reduce health. A short damage cooldown prevents multiple damage events from
removing all health in a single instant.

When health reaches zero, one life is lost. If lives remain, the player
respawns with full health at the latest collected-crystal checkpoint. Losing
all three lives causes a game over.

The HUD tracks player health, lives, score, Core health, Grid integrity,
missed shots, Anchor progress, memory progress, equipment charges, hostiles,
Core-link progress, objective direction, and survival time.


10. FIELD HAZARDS AND COLLISION
-------------------------------

Mechanical spike traps contain nine spikes and operate on timed cycles:

- Amber lights indicate that a trap is preparing or retracted.
- Red lights and raised spikes indicate danger.
- Active spikes cause heavy damage when the player is close to the ground.
- The player can cross when the spikes are retracted, move around the plate,
  or jump over it.

Environmental collision also applies to buildings, fallen structures, rubble,
trees, map boundaries, and other solid field objects. These objects provide
cover and influence enemy pathfinding.


11. VISUAL EFFECTS AND GAME STATES
-----------------------------------

The OpenGL rendering system includes:

- Neon cyan, magenta, red, and green color themes.
- Animated Rift Anchors and rotating memory crystals.
- Floating enemies with animated attacks and stun indicators.
- Energy projectiles and hostile corruption projectiles.
- EMP rings and particle bursts.
- Floating score text and hit markers.
- Muzzle flashes and weapon recoil.
- Weapon bob and sway during movement.
- Damage flashes and camera shake.
- Health bars and progress bars.
- Fog, sky effects, terrain decoration, and lighting-style color feedback.

The game includes playing, paused, game-over, victory, restart, and
ending-choice states.


12. BRANCHING STORY ENDINGS
---------------------------

After the final Core link reaches 100%, the player chooses ECHO's fate:

Press 1 — Destroy ECHO
    Restore the physical cities, but sacrifice the stored digital memories.
    Ending screen: CITIES RESTORED.

Press 2 — Merge with ECHO
    Merge Aegis-7 with ECHO and preserve humanity's digital past.
    Ending screen: MEMORIES PRESERVED.

Both choices complete the game and award 500 points.


13. WIN AND LOSS CONDITIONS
---------------------------

The player wins by:

- Destroying all three Rift Anchors.
- Recovering all three ECHO memory crystals.
- Returning to the Grid Core.
- Completing the 24-second Core link.
- Selecting one of the two endings.

The player loses if:

- All three Guardian lives are exhausted.
- Rift Beasts reduce Core health to zero.
- Missed projectiles reduce Grid integrity to zero.
